Circle of Competence

Only analyze businesses you can explain simply.

Questions to answer

  • What does the company sell, to whom, and why do customers buy?
  • What are the 2–3 key profit drivers — price, volume, retention, cost structure?
  • What could permanently damage the business (not just a bad quarter)?
2

The Moat

Evidence that profits can stay high despite competition.

Moat types — mark what applies

Evidence to collect (10-year view if possible)

  • Stable or improving gross margin and operating margin
  • Stable or improving market share without margin collapse
  • Ability to raise prices without losing customers
3

Economic Engine

The company turns capital into cash at attractive rates.

Key metrics (quality checks)

Red flags to rule out

  • Earnings up, but FCF weak for many years
  • Heavy share dilution funding operations
  • Returns only look good because of debt
4

Management

Rational, honest, shareholder-minded decisions.

What to check

  • Candor in annual letters — are mistakes acknowledged?
  • Incentives aligned with long-term value

Valuation

Pay a fair price — or better — for a wonderful business.

6.1 Owner earnings — practical estimate

  1. Start with operating cash flow
  2. Subtract maintenance capex — what's needed to maintain competitive position
  3. Adjust for unusual, one-off items

6.2 Intrinsic value range

Conservative growth assumptions. Discount rate you're comfortable with — often 8–12%. A range, not a point.
